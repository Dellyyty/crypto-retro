About · v1.0

A market broadcast from 1993.

CRYPTOCAST renders live crypto market data inside the visual grammar of a WeatherStar-era cable broadcast — the local forecast segment on The Weather Channel before the internet ate the screen. It is a browser-only artifact: no backend, no build step, no tracking, no API keys, no fabricated numbers.

Not financial advice. CRYPTOCAST reports public market data. It does not produce signals, predictions, or recommendations. Numbers on screen are as fresh as the upstream API and are labeled with their source on the Data page.

Why this exists

Weather broadcasts from the late 1980s and early 1990s were a specific kind of interface design: dense, high-contrast, information-first, and unapologetically ugly by modern standards. Every pixel carried a reading. Nothing was decorative except the gradient header and the icon glyphs.

Crypto dashboards went the other direction — glassmorphism, purple glow, whitespace, and a candlestick chart per viewport. The information density collapsed while the visual noise increased. CRYPTOCAST is the counterfactual: what if a market terminal had been designed by the people who built WeatherStar III, where a screen holds twenty numbers and you can read all of them in four seconds from across a room?

The result is also a genuinely fast dashboard. It ships as static files, boots from a cached snapshot in under one frame, and works offline after first load.

What's on the broadcast

1 · Conditions

The headline: BTC price, the market's "weather" derived from 24h change, range, dominance, volume, sentiment, gas, funding, hashrate.

2 · Market Ladder

Top twelve assets with last, 24h, 7d, and a seven-day sparkline. Everything on one screen, no scrolling.

3 · Candle Range

Daily candles for BTC/ETH/SOL from Coinbase spot, plus 1D/7D/30D change cards at the bottom, laid out like the five-day forecast.

4 · Chain Telemetry

Bitcoin block height, mempool backlog, three fee tiers, hashrate, difficulty and retarget progress; Ethereum gas and base fee; perpetual funding for BTC and ETH.

5 · Market Ephemeris

Fear & Greed gauge, dominance bars, and the real moon phases — the one piece of the broadcast that is genuinely astronomical rather than metaphorical.

6 · Discussion

The narrative screen, written the way a local forecaster would read it: short declarative sentences, no adjectives that imply a trade. Plus the halving countdown, computed from the block height.

Every screen has a URL fragment (#conditions, #markets, #chart, #network, #ephemeris, #discussion). Appending ?direct skips the title card — useful for embedding: index.html?direct#network.

What it deliberately is not

  • Not real-time. Markets refresh once a minute; chain data twice a minute. Anything faster is a lie about what public APIs will give you for free.
  • Not an exchange view. Prices come from aggregated spot data; they will not match your venue's order book to the cent.
  • Not personalized. No accounts, no cookies, no analytics. The only storage is a local snapshot cache so the first paint is instant.
  • Not a trading tool. No order entry, no alerts, no wallets.

Provenance

All data is public, keyless and CORS-enabled. Specific endpoints, refresh cadence, and failure behavior are documented on Data. The design system, including why the palette is exactly four colors, is on Design. The deploy topology is on Infra.
