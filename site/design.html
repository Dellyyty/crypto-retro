Design · system

Four colors, two type sizes.

The reference is a 1990s cable weather segment: purple field, orange header gradient, navy data panel, yellow captions, black keylines. CRYPTOCAST keeps that system and adds exactly one thing — a semantic up/down pair. Nothing else. The constraint is the design.

Green and red are the only additions, and they are muted by broadcast standards — not exchange green. They never appear as fills, only as text and sparkline strokes, so the board does not turn into a traffic light. Cyan is a label color, never a data color.

Type

One family, variable in weight and width. Condensed for density, the wider cut only for the small uppercase metadata, exactly as the reference does it. All figures are font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums; without that the board twitches every time a digit changes, which reads as a glitch.

Headings carry a half-pixel black stroke (-webkit-text-stroke) plus a hard offset shadow. That is what makes yellow on navy legible at distance without resorting to a heavier weight.

Geometry

  • Stage: 4:3, letterboxed on black, scaled with min(100vw, 133.333dvh).
  • Rows: 18.75% masthead / 1fr body / 16.6667% ticker — the proportions of the original.
  • Panel inset: the data panel floats inside a gradient body with an inner white glow, so it reads as a lit screen behind glass.
  • Keylines: 1 px black on every panel join; forecast cards use a 0.16 em white border with an inset black border, the classic double-rule.
  • Radii: zero, except the start-screen button. Nothing in the broadcast is rounded.

Icons

All glyphs are original SVG on a 48×48 grid, 2.5 px black strokes, flat fills, and a single hard drop shadow. They are chunky because they were designed to survive NTSC composite video: thin lines would smear into the background. The sun/moon split, cloud silhouette, and rain marks are conventions of the form, drawn from scratch rather than traced.

FlickerThree-frame opacity jitter, 7.3 s cycle, 4% of the time—

The wipe is stepped, not eased. Continuous easing reads as modern UI; broadcast hardware had a frame budget and the stepper is the tell. The turn-on animation is the one place a curve is allowed, because CRT phosphor response is continuous.

Motion accessibility

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce kills the turn-on transform, the wipes, the marquee, the flicker and the cloud drift. Data updates continue. The one animation that survives is the ticker's static content — without scroll it becomes a single readable line, so the information is never lost to the preference.

Layout rules

  1. No scrolling. A broadcast cannot scroll. If content does not fit, it is cut or the type gets smaller — never a scrollbar.
  2. One number per row. Labels left, values right, always on the same baseline. No centered data tables.
  3. Yellow means "look here." At most three yellow elements per screen. If everything is captioned, nothing is.
  4. Color is never the only signal. Change carries a sign (+/−) and an arrow in the ticker, so the up/down pair is redundant.
  5. Every panel has an edge. No floating cards on a gradient. The physicality is the point.
  6. Density over whitespace. Twelve assets on one screen at 0.68 em beats four assets at 1.2 em. This is the entire thesis.

Contrast

White on #354e84 is 7.4:1; yellow on #354e84 is 8.9:1. Both exceed WCAG AA for body text and AAA for large text. The up/down pair was darkened from the obvious neon to #7DFF8A / #FF8F8F so it clears 4.5:1 on the navy panel while still reading as "bright" on a CRT. Focus rings are 2 px yellow with a 2 px offset, visible on every background in the system.
